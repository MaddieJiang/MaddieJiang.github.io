👋 About Me

I'm Madeleine Jiang, an incoming 11th grader at California Academy of Mathematics and Science. I've always been drawn to creative work, whether that's through art, music, or designing and building things with my hands, and that ultimately led me into STEM. Robotics let me combine creativity with problem solving, and I've been pursuing that combination ever since, each project at a time.

Along the way I've realized how deeply my personal hobbies and experiences have shaped the way my projects turn out, and that what I actually care about isn't just making something that works, it's making something that feels thought through, almost like design in its own right. Now I'm using what I've learned to visit local organizations and see if there are everyday problems I could help solve with something I build myself.

🚀 The Initiative

The Problem

Local organizations deal with a lot of small, everyday tasks that still get done by hand: counting donations, logging inventory, keeping track of stock. Nobody questions it much, it's just how things have always been done, but a lot of these tasks could be made easier with smart environments.

Smart environments are everyday objects with a small computer and a way to connect, so they can sense what's happening and act on their own instead of waiting for someone to check by hand. A smart speaker listens and responds, a smart lock knows when to open, it's less about the object itself and more about giving ordinary things a way to notice something and tell you about it.

That's the same idea behind what I build, just aimed at solving one specific operational challenge at a time. I don't walk in with a device already in mind, I work with what a client actually needs and design around it. A shelf that tracks its own inventory might work well for most items, but not every item fits a standard shelf, so the research and prototyping is what lets me adjust the form to fit the problem instead of the other way around.

The Approach

For all 8 years I've spent in robotics, I've used the same steps: define the problem, sketch out ideas, build a prototype, test it, and adjust based on what happens. Every step is recorded in detail, sketches, test results, notes on what to change, because that documentation is what drives the next decision.

Where It Stands

Currently in the research stage, reaching out to local organizations to learn about their day to day processes and find out where a device could actually help. No prototype yet, as that comes after I understand the actual problem well enough to design for it.

What's Next
  • Define — keep reaching out to local organizations to find real problems worth solving
  • Prototype — build a working device based on what they actually need
  • Test — bring it on site and see how it holds up in real use
  • Iterate — adjust based on what actually happens, not what I assumed
  • Document — write up the process so the next project builds on the last
